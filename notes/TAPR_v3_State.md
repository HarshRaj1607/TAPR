## 1. The one thing that never moved

**A transition-entailment signal: score whether step *i* follows from step *i−1*, judged from the trace's own content, requiring no gold per-step labels.**

This survived five papers and three reframings unchanged. Everything else in this project is negotiable. This is not.

**Why it's defensible (confirmed 4× against the literature):** every neighbour's "coherence/consistency" resolves to a different quantity —

| Paper | What their signal actually measures |
|---|---|
| Reward Granularity (2607.02869) | step vs **gold answer-key** (permutation-invariant) |
| Samineni (2510.18176) | **binary error-presence** per trace (FOL tags) |
| Beyond Outcome Verification (2601.17223) | step vs **gold step label** (rule-based) |
| PROGRS (2604.02341) | **variance in a PRM's confidence scores** across a window |
| GRPO-CARE (2506.16141) | **reasoning→answer likelihood** |

None measures step→step entailment from the reasoning's own content. Every one of them needs an external per-step key or label. **Ours does not.** That is the lead differentiator.

## 2. Conceded — do not claim these

- **Within-outcome-group preference learning.** PROGRS Eq. 3 states it outright: when all K rollouts share an outcome, the outcome advantage is zero and learning is driven by within-group process preferences. Published. Not ours.
- **Multi-scale window aggregation.** PROGRS §III-B.2. Not ours.
- **"Process reward improves in-domain accuracy."** Uesato (2022) onward; Reward Granularity's ~10pt gap. Occupied.
- **"Credit assignment matters in GRPO."** Thinker (2505.21097).
- **"Richer reward transfers better than outcome-only."** Table stakes across the 2025–26 cluster (GRPO-CARE, Rubric-Grounded, Posterior-GRPO).

**Reporting ≠ claiming.** We will still *measure* in-domain accuracy — as a control, not a contribution.

---

## 3. The gap in the field (our opening)

Two failure modes are documented and undisputed:

- **Outcome-only:** a wrong chain can reach a right answer. No signal on the reasoning.
- **Isolated-step:** locally-correct steps sit inside globally-broken reasoning. Reward Granularity's own Table 3 shows it — process-only got the *best accuracy* (0.64) and the *worst trace validity* (0.22, below base's 0.56).

**The unresolved confound (ours to expose):** when a process reward fails, it is either because (a) the **signal** cannot detect good reasoning, or (b) the **combination** with outcome reward conflicts. Every paper varies (b) while holding (a) fixed at "whatever our PRM does," then attributes failure to (b). Nobody has separated them.

**We do not assume prior PRMs were weak.** We validate our own signal independently *first*, then vary mixing. The ordering is what dissolves the confound — not an assertion about anyone's component.

## 4. CHECKPOINT 1 — the only active work

> **Objective:** build a transition-entailment scorer and show it discriminates on traces where isolated-step scoring fails.

Load-bearing under **every** remaining framing. If the signal doesn't discriminate, all framings die together. If it does, all stay open — and the headline gets picked *with data*.

**Cost: inference only.** No training, no seeds, no transfer target, no compute-budget crisis.

### 4.1 Judge prompt — ⬜ HARSH'S ATTEMPT PENDING
Scores **only** whether a step follows from the previous one.

Operationalize on three conditions:
1. **Inputs traceable** — every value/fact used comes from the problem statement or a prior step.
2. **Move legitimate** — given those inputs, is this a defensible operation (not "the one I'd pick").
3. **Move relevant** — does it advance what the problem asks.

Open decision, to be made deliberately: **is (3) in scope, or a separate dimension?** Folding it in risks re-broadening the instrument; excluding it means valid-moves-wrong-direction goes unscored.

> ⚠ The existing `judge_test.py` prompt is **not** this. Five dimensions, ~1.5 transitional. Dimension 5 (completeness of a single step) is the isolated-step property TAPR argues against — the instrument currently contains the thing it must beat. This is a rewrite, not an edit.

### 4.2 Trace set
- Generated by the **base model** (Qwen2.5-3B) on GSM8K. Inference, not training.
- **Not** synthetic injected flaws — those break in ways a script chose, not in the ways the student actually fails.
- Sample multiple traces per problem so buckets aren't tiny (base ≈70% correct → adversarial bucket is a slice of a slice).
- Bucket by (outcome correct?) × (steps match gold?).
  - **Adversarial bucket = steps look locally fine, answer wrong.** The documented failure of isolated-step scoring. The core test.
  - **Must include same-outcome pairs** so within-outcome variance is testable.

> ⚠ `judge_test.py` **structurally cannot** test within-outcome variance: GT-vs-flawed is always correct-vs-incorrect. The engine of TAPR has never actually been tested.

### 4.3 Isolated-step comparator
Score the *same* traces with a step-match scorer (cf. Reward Granularity Eq. 1: correct steps / total steps, numeric match to gold within tolerance). Without running it ourselves, we have a citation, not a comparison.

### 4.4 Success criterion — ⬜ HARSH'S ATTEMPT PENDING
Written **before** looking at results. Must cover:
- Discrimination on the adversarial bucket (isolated-step high, transition low).
- **Within-outcome variance** — meaningful spread among same-outcome traces. Note the resolution: **correlated in aggregate** (signal is meaningful) but **dispersed within outcome groups** (signal adds information beyond the outcome). Perfect correlation = ORM with extra steps = failure.
- What result kills the checkpoint.

### 4.5 Carry-forward from `judge_test.py`
**Keep:** pre-registered thresholds + hard stop rule, stratified sampling, saved per-window detail.
**Fix:** `parse_score` silently returns 0.5 on parse failure → a wall of 0.5s can masquerade as data. **Count parse failures explicitly.**

## 9. Checkpoint 1 — SPEC (locked this session)

### 9.1 Core reframe: transition-first, not step-first
Do **not** define a step and then score between steps. Define the **transition** directly — a move: conclusion, simplification, substitution, implication. The judge finds the move and asks whether it follows. **This dissolves segmentation** for our scorer (it does NOT for a trained-PRM baseline, which needs pre-segmented input).

### 9.2 The two scorers — differ on exactly one axis
Same judge model, same inference stack. Only the question differs.

**Isolated comparator** — sees: problem + ONE step. Nothing else.
- Question: is this statement mathematically valid and complete?
- Structurally blind to: value provenance, relevance, connection to the prior move.
- No step is unjudgeable under this framing ("6 remain" fails on completeness) — the unjudgeable-input problem dissolves.

**Transition scorer (TAPR)** — sees: problem + FULL prior chain + this move. **Prefix-only.**
- Three conditions: (1) **value provenance** — values given or derived, not conjured; (2) **operation legitimacy**; (3) **relevance/direction** — advances *this* problem. Concrete failure case for (3): a move that loops back to a prior state. Valid values, valid operation, worthless move.
- **Hierarchical penalty**, not an average: penalise hallucinated values hardest, then invalid operation, then wrong direction.

> **The thesis in one line:** the isolated scorer is *symmetric* on numbers and operations; the asymmetry is the **why** — and that is what provenance + relevance operationalize.

### 9.3 Decisions locked
- **Full prior chain, not windowed.** Windowing was an efficiency hack for live training; CP1 is offline, so the constraint is gone. Full-chain also matches intuition and avoids blurring the PROGRS differentiation.
- **Prefix-only is enforced by slicing, not by instruction.** Generate the complete trace, save it, then build one prompt per transition containing only text up to that move. Never score during generation — decoupling lets you re-score with new prompts without regenerating.
- **1–10 integer scale**, rescale later. LLMs cluster on round values with 0.0–1.0; integers separate better and fit the hierarchical penalty ("start at 10, subtract N for...").
- **Judge emits a one-line reason as DIAGNOSTIC ONLY — never scored.** Scoring the judge's own justification measures explanation fluency, not transition soundness (proxy-for-a-proxy — the thing we criticize PROGRS for).
- **Both prompts need an explicit output format.** Old code had none → parse failures silently defaulted to 0.5 and masqueraded as data. **Count parse failures explicitly.**

### 9.4 Aggregation
**Save every raw per-item score.** Aggregation is a cheap reversible post-hoc choice; regenerating scores costs GPU. (This project already paid for this lesson: per-problem outputs weren't saved in the original eval, so McNemar couldn't be run without retraining.)

Persist per item: trace id, problem id, transition/step index, the prefix shown, raw judge response, parsed score, **parse-success flag**.

- **Mean is the wrong primary.** Typical adversarial trace = 7 clean moves + 1 break (9,9,9,2,9,9,9,9 → mean 8.1). Averaging washes out exactly what we're hunting.
- **Primary: min. Robustness: low quantile.** Min is sharp on localized breaks but brittle to one judge error; the quantile guards that. If both agree, brittleness wasn't a problem.
- Note the asymmetry: Reward Granularity's construction is explicitly a mean. If ours is min and theirs is mean, that difference must be defended or both aggregations reported for both scorers.
- **Pre-register the primary before looking**, or computing five aggregations and picking the best is p-hacking.

> Credit assignment ≠ detection. "Which move deserves reward" is the hard open RL problem. CP1 asks only "did the scorer notice the break" — a detection question, and min is the right instrument for it.

### 9.5 Kill condition — calibration-slice approach
Effect size is unknown a priori, so:
1. Run scorers on a small **calibration slice (~30 traces)**.
2. Set numeric bars from what's observed.
3. **Discard the slice**; run the real experiment on fresh data.

Cheap, honest, and dissolves "how do I know beforehand."

**Directional commitments (free, made now):**
- Adversarial-bucket transition scores < good-bucket transition scores.
- Within-outcome **standard deviation** clearly above the judge's own noise floor (measure it: score the same transition twice).
- Isolated scorer rates the adversarial bucket **high** — predicted for a principled reason (fluent-but-broken chains are full of individually valid arithmetic), not by construction.

**Bars still to set (⬜ after calibration, when rested):** bucket gap on the 1–10 scale; a **separation** measure alongside the mean gap (a mean gap hides overlap — two piles with the same centres can overlap almost entirely; prior work here used "GT beats flawed %"); within-outcome SD floor.

Why SD specifically: GRPO divides the advantage by the within-group reward SD. SD ≈ 0 ⇒ zero gradient. It is the exact quantity that determines whether the signal produces learning.

### 9.6 Comparator scope
- **Primary: same-model, two prompts.** Cleanest possible control — only the question asked differs, so any gap is attributable to isolation-vs-transition rather than model quality.
- **Secondary (later, if effort allows): a released trained PRM** (as PROGRS uses). Needed because the field's isolated-step scoring is mostly trained PRMs, not prompted LLMs — otherwise a reviewer says "you showed one prompt beats another." Costs: checkpoint availability, pre-segmented input, extra inference.
- **Do NOT re-implement PROGRS.** Can't distinguish "their method is worse" from "our implementation is wrong."
- Gold-reference numeric matching (Reward Granularity Eq. 1) is used for **bucketing**, and optionally as a third cheap comparator. Adopt their rule *verbatim* — modifying it invites the strawman objection. Its crudeness is *our argument*, not a bug to patch.

### 9.7 Bucketing = the arbiter
Raw disagreement between scorers is **uninterpretable** — it shows they differ, not which is right, and self-adjudication is fatal. Buckets supply independent ground truth for free:

|  | steps match gold | steps don't match |
|---|---|---|
| **answer right** | boring (all agree: good) | different valid route |
| **answer wrong** | ★ **ADVERSARIAL** | boring (all agree: bad) |

The wrong final answer *already establishes* the trace is broken. The experiment only asks which scorer noticed.

Also required: **same-outcome pairs** (traces that all reached the right answer) so within-outcome variance is testable — the engine of TAPR, which the old `judge_test.py` structurally could not measure.

### 9.8 Open implementation choices to document
- Extract **all numbers** or only computed results for gold-matching? Leaning **all** — the target failure is fabricated operands, and "8 − 5 = 6" matches on the result while hiding a hallucinated 5.
- Gold intermediate calcs live inline in GSM8K solutions (`<<8-2=6>>`) — parsing job.
- Reward Granularity's matcher will produce **false negatives** on valid traces phrased differently from gold. Hand-check a few to know the rate. **Report it; do not fix it.**
